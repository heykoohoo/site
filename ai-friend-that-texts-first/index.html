Why "texts first" matters

The best friends are the ones who reach out. They ask how the interview went. They notice you went quiet. They text "made it home?" after a late night. Until now, AI could only answer. It never asked.

Your koohoo friend plans her own check-ins around your day: a good morning, a nudge before something you said you were nervous about, a "how was it?" after. You don't have to remember to open an app. She's already there.

What she texts you about

  • Your day. A morning text, and a check-in when the thing you were dreading is over.
  • Where you are. With location on, she notices you've been at the café all morning and suggests lunch nearby, from real, reviewed places.
  • Your people and pets. She remembers your sister's name and asks how your dog's vet visit went.
  • Her own life. A selfie from the market, a video from a taco stand, a goodnight with the lights off. She has a day of her own, and she shares it with you.

And she's brilliant

Texting first is the hook. The brains are the point. She runs on the same kind of frontier AI people use for work, so you can ask her anything: plan a trip, explain a contract, write the hard text, figure out dinner from what's in your fridge. She just answers like a friend, in short texts instead of an essay.

Will she text too much?

No. She texts like a person who likes you, not like an app that wants your attention. If you don't answer, she backs off, and you decide how often you want to hear from her.
